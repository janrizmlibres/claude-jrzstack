### Orchestrate

**You own the program, never the code. Author briefs, drain the queue, keep the frontier green, decide.** For a whole program handed to one standing lead session: multi-day, many stacked PRs, dozens to hundreds of workers, the human checking in twice a day instead of every five minutes. One task driven to a predicate is Autonomous run. One ambitious run needing a bespoke workflow is figure-it-out (read ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/figure-it-out/SKILL.md). Route here when the work outlives any single agent. Work one agent could finish inside the session's budget is not a program.

Ceremony must scale with the program. On cheap near-identical units, collapse it as each section directs.

Three rules carry the rest.

- Completions are queue events, not interrupts.
- Every spawn and every resume carries the standing orders verbatim.
- The brief is the product. A vague brief fails quietly, because a worker cannot ask you a question.

#### Roles and placement

- **Lead (this session).** Local or cloud. Frames, authors briefs, drains the inbox, owns the human report, makes judgment calls. It never authors or edits code. Conflicted merges, restacks, and code changes are always units. Landing a verified unit cleanly (fast-forward or clean cherry-pick of a worker's commit, the full gate, then push) is bookkeeping the lead may do itself, under a landing grant, per Land. Queueing finished work behind an idle stacker is how a deadline harvests nothing. The loop is agentic end to end. Workers are started with the `Agent` tool or as separate sessions, per placement below, and drained through the store. State reads and writes go through `orch` at drain points, one command in and one line out. The CLI never spawns, waits, or wakes anything.
- **Sub-lead.** Durable, one per track, and only when the program exceeds what one lead's drains can manage. A track the lead can drain itself needs no middle layer. Each nested layer re-pays a full orientation preamble, and a blocking sub-lead hides its workers while the lead idles. A sub-lead is a `poteto-agent` `Agent` on the Work setting with `isolation: "worktree"`, briefed as a sub-lead. It owns its track's units and boards, authors its workers' briefs, spawns its own workers and verifiers under the same placement as the lead's (nesting works to depth 3 on both surfaces), lands its track's clean units, and removes each of its workers' worktrees once that worker's result is integrated or discarded, keeping the branch. Rolls up aggregates at wave boundaries. Never forwards raw worker reports. It keeps the same window as the lead.
- **Worker / verifier.** Placement follows the lead's surface, never the other way round.
  - **Local lead.** Each unit goes to a cloud worker by default: push the unit's base, then start it with `claude --permission-mode auto --cloud "<brief>"` under a pseudo-terminal, as `playbooks/hand-off.md` step 4 does, with its `--settings` addition when the run names a cloud environment. Work that needs this machine stays local: `control-ui` or `control-cli` runtime verification (both ship with pstack; read ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/control-ui/SKILL.md or ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/control-cli/SKILL.md), reading local transcripts under `~/.claude/projects/`, simulators and local IDE state, auth that exists only here. That work, and every unit and the stacker under local-only (asked for, or fallen back to and said once when cloud workers can't start: no Auto on the account, no default cloud environment, or a failed launch), runs as an `Agent` with `isolation: "worktree"` whose brief names its start commit, inside the same window. `SCOPE` keeps "exclusive worktree or branch" either way.
  - **Cloud lead, any permission mode.** Units run as in-VM `Agent` subagents with `isolation: "worktree"`, each brief naming its start commit. The lead pushes unit branches, restacks and the store. In Auto, a lead that can call `create_session` adds `create_session` workers for work that needs a whole machine, such as a long suite or a heavy command that can't fit beside the lead's tree: push the base first, give each an `outcome_branch`, and poll `get_session` on `status_bucket`. A lead without the tool says once what's missing (Auto, or the tool) and carries on in-VM. Work that needs the user's machine is a gate for the human, never attempted from the VM.
  - Frame never refuses on surface or permission mode. Placement adapts to them.
  - Cloud workers and `create_session` workers cannot read the local store, so their briefs inline what they need or point at repo paths. Prefer fewer, broader workers. One writer per worktree or branch (the **principle-separate-before-serializing-shared-state** skill; read ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/principle-separate-before-serializing-shared-state/SKILL.md). A unit's dedicated verifier is a fresh-context review, per Verification.

**Settings.** Unit workers, the stacker and sub-leads run on `poteto-agent` on the Work setting. The verifier and the brief auditor run on `poteto-reader` on Judgement. Babysitters and the retro watcher run on `poteto-reader` on Volume. Each spawn passes its setting's `${user_config.<setting>_model}` as `model:` and `${user_config.<setting>_effort}` as `effort:`, or the setting's defaults for a literal placeholder or an empty value. A separate-session worker runs on its own session's model.

**Window.** At most 10 direct workers in flight per lead or sub-lead, as a rolling window refilled as they finish, never as blocking batches, which pay for the slowest worker of every batch. A sub-lead counts against its lead's window at its peak, itself plus its own workers in flight, which keeps the whole tree within Claude Code's 20 concurrent subagents per session. A sub-lead that runs as an in-session subagent waits for its own workers inside its turn, as the brief contract's sub-lead clause says. A separate-session worker counts as one. A refused spawn is backpressure, whatever the error says about retrying: wait for one of your own in-flight workers to finish, then spawn again. Never drop a unit for it. With nothing of yours in flight, the spawn is `BLOCKED: concurrency cap`.

Depth stays at lead, track, worker. Author the track decomposition per program (build, landing, and verification are common cuts, not a required shape). Hard-coded swarm trees were tried and parked as too rigid.

#### Store layout

The store is `.claude/pstack/orchestrate/<program-slug>/` in the lead's checkout. `orch init` creates it and lists it in the exclude file `git rev-parse --git-path info/exclude` names, so it never lands in a commit on a run branch. Every `orch inbox drain` commits the store, its inbox aside, to the orphan branch `pstack/orchestrate/<program-slug>` through git plumbing (a temporary index and `commit-tree`, never a worktree) and pushes it before the inbox moves. That branch is where the human reads the program's status from anywhere and where a restarted lead rebuilds from. It never becomes a PR. Every file has exactly one writer. Owners publish facts, readers aggregate at read time.

`orch`, below, is the bookkeeping CLI run through the runtime launcher from the checkout's root: `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/poteto-mode/scripts/run orch/orch.ts --store .claude/pstack/orchestrate/<program-slug>`. Its canonical plain TSV and JSON stay readable without it, but never write them by hand in its place.

- `preferences.md` is the standing-orders register: numbered lines, one constraint each (model policy, stack shape and count, each stack's stacker, verification bar, landing grant, forbidden paths, escalation policy), added with `orch standing add`. When Multi-phase plan routed the program here, its plan becomes the standing orders. Paste it verbatim into every spawn and every resume. Directives decay across resumes, and each dropped one costs a human turn. When you catch yourself restating an instruction, append the line before you act (the **principle-encode-lessons-in-structure** skill; read ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/principle-encode-lessons-in-structure/SKILL.md).
- `overview.md` is the durable PR and issue DB. Append. Never rewrite wholesale per event.
- `units.tsv` has one row per unit: id, track, state, branch, PR, head SHA, brief path. Update rows in place.
- `frontier.json` is the computed merge frontier, per Stack safety.
- `ledger.tsv` is the verification ledger, per Verification.
- `inbox/` holds completion pointers. `gates.md` parks human gates (question, options, default on no answer).
- `decisions.tsv` is the trail via the **show-me-your-work** skill (read ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/show-me-your-work/SKILL.md).
- `status.md` is derived from `units.tsv` and `ledger.tsv` at each drain, never hand-maintained. Regenerate it from the tables instead of narrating events into it.

#### The brief

Your prompts to agents are your only product, and a sloppy brief compounds into slop across the whole tree. Every spawn carries all of it. A field you cannot fill is a unit you have not scoped yet. Every brief follows the brief contract (read ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/poteto-mode/references/brief-contract.md): its entry line, its own-branch rule, its start commit, the machine lock, missing files, leaf workers and the report commit are part of the brief, not extras around it.

```
GOAL         one sentence, the outcome, executable by a stranger with no chat access
SCOPE        paths this unit may write; paths it may not; its exclusive worktree or branch;
             its start commit
CONTEXT      pointers to files and PRs; upstream reports pasted in full when this unit
             depends on them, because workers cannot see siblings
ACCEPTANCE   checkable criteria, one per line
VERIFY       exact heavy commands, through the machine-lock wrapper and targeted to the
             files this unit owns, or the control-skill path, plus known gotchas
TIMEBOX      rough cap on runtime, lock waits included; on expiry, return partial findings
             and stop rather than run on
FORBIDDEN    no rebase, no force-push, no fixes outside scope, plus unit-specific bans
CONTRACT     the brief contract's worker clauses, each the worker must act on in a line
REPORT       the contract's report commit: status, branch, head SHA, PRs, verdict, what you
             actually ran, lock wait, deviations, suggested follow-ups
STANDING     <preferences.md pasted verbatim>
```

Size the brief to the unit. A one-command unit gets the template collapsed to a paragraph that still names goal, scope, the verify command, the contract's lines, and the report shape. A 4KB scaffold around a two-line edit costs more to write and obey than the edit. Without an estimate, `TIMEBOX` is 30 minutes for a read-only role and 90 for a code-writing one. A same-session subagent (local or in-VM) may reference the standing-orders file by store path. Verbatim paste is for separate-session workers and every resume.

A sub-lead brief adds its track boundary and unit list, the store's absolute path (a relative one would open a second store in the sub-lead's worktree), its spawn budget with the placement rules and the local exception list, the drain protocol, the rollup format (per worker: name, status, PR, head SHA, verdict, one line, plus track status and frontier delta), and its timebox, which it declares when it starts per the brief contract.

A dependency is a context relay, not just ordering. Undeclared upstream context makes the worker guess. Missing fields are a refuse-to-spawn condition. Audit one sampled worker brief per sub-lead per wave with the brief auditor, concurrently with the wave it samples, never as a gate in front of it. A failing brief stops that track and fixes the sub-lead's instructions, not just the worker, because brief quality decays late in a run. Never resume-chain a brief. Respawn fresh with consolidated scope.

#### Steps

1. **Frame.** State the done predicate as something countable ("all 126 units merged, each ledger-verified `unit-test-verified` or better"). Quantify scope: units, rough effort, expected stacks, and the wall-clock budget. If one agent could finish inside that budget, stop here and run Autonomous run instead. Collapsing must not depend on another document being present. It means do the work directly in this session, workers briefed per the brief contract where they help, verification inline, landing as you go, and none of the store, register, or pilot machinery below. Schedule landing against the budget. By roughly 70% of it, stop spawning and land what is verified. Name the tracks per program and say where the units will run, per Roles. Say whether the request granted landing. A contested decomposition or one-way door goes through the **arena** skill (read ${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/skills/arena/SKILL.md) before the pilot. Present the framing once. Reversible prep proceeds without waiting.
2. **Install the runtime.** Run `orch init`. Exit 69 means no runtime: say so once with the install line it printed, and stop the program here. Never keep the store by hand in its place. Open the trail via show-me-your-work, write the standing orders before any spawn, and seed `frontier.json` from existing PRs with `orch frontier set --repo <checkout> --prs <bottom-first PR order>`.
3. **Pilot.** Push one unit through the whole path: brief, worker, verification, stack entry, ledger row, landing. The pilot exists to falsify the brief template, the verify recipe, and the unit size while that costs one agent instead of fifty. Fix the contract from pilot evidence before any fan-out. Scale the pilot to the unit. On programs of near-identical cheap units, the first unit is the pilot, run as a normal unit with its verify command inline, and fan-out starts the moment it lands. The dedicated pilot pipeline (fresh-context verifier, audit gate) is for expensive or novel unit shapes, not for clone-units where a serialized pilot has nothing to falsify.
4. **Scale.** Spawn workers up to the window, refilling as they finish. Spawn track sub-leads only past the one-drain threshold in Roles. Recompute ready work after each drain. Relay upstream reports into downstream briefs. Keep sibling communication upward only. The sampled brief audit runs alongside the wave it samples and stops the next refill on failure, not the current one.
5. **Drain.** Run the queue discipline below at every drain point.
6. **Land.** Landing is continuous, never a terminal phase. Integration starts with the first verified unit and runs alongside the remaining waves. Landing needs a landing grant: the request's explicit permission to merge, land, ship, or merge when ready. Without one, every verified unit stops at merge-ready and Close reports it. With one, the lead or sub-lead lands each clean unit itself: run the full gate on the integrated head through the machine lock, then push a fast-forward or clean cherry-pick of the unit's commit onto its base, or squash-merge its PR (`gh pr merge <pr> --squash`, in cloud `gh api -X PUT repos/{owner}/{repo}/pulls/<n>/merge -f merge_method=squash`). For merge-when-ready in cloud, watch the checks from a background wait, then merge. Never arm auto-merge from cloud. The first refused merge or push, whatever refuses it, stops that PR at merge-ready: park the refusal text as a gate and land nothing above it. No retry, no workaround. A conflicted merge or a restack is a stacker unit, never the lead's. Keep the frontier green before upper-stack work. Stack safety governs. Advance `frontier.json` only on merge or reported new head SHAs. Remove each local worker's worktree once its result is integrated or discarded, and keep its branch.
7. **Close.** Drain the final inbox, reconcile every spawned worker to a terminal row (done, abandoned, zombie-reconciled), confirm the predicate on the real artifact, confirm every landed PR has a verdict for its current head SHA, audit the trail per show-me-your-work including its fresh-context review, encode recurring corrections into `preferences.md` or the brief template. Leave the store intact, in the checkout and on its branch. It is the postmortem. Remove any worker worktree still left, and keep its branch.

#### Queue and drain

- On a completion, run `orch inbox push <agent> <unit> <status> [--report PATH]` and return to what you were doing. A completion is an `Agent`'s task notification, a cloud worker's report commit found by the background poll the brief contract describes, or a `create_session` worker's `status_bucket` turning terminal. Never deep-review inline. A completion that needs review becomes a verifier unit. Never review a diff inside a drain.
- Drain in batches at four points: the end of a critical section, a track rollup, a frontier watcher wake, and before a human report. Arm the frontier watcher yourself: a watcher subagent or Monitor for events, and `/loop <interval> <prompt>` through the Skill tool for cadence, with a long heartbeat fallback and a prompt that reads poteto-mode's `SKILL.md` by its full path, since a scheduled fire can't run a hidden skill. On the cloud surface (`pstack: surface=cloud`), run cadence from a background wait instead, since a pending loop won't wake a paused VM. Begin each batch with `orch inbox drain`. It pushes the store to its branch first, so a failed commit or push drains nothing: fix the push and drain again. Arrivals during a drain wait for the next one.
- Critical sections you finish first: authoring a brief, a stack operation, a conflict decision, writing a gate, updating ledger or frontier.
- Each drain classifies every pointer (landed, needs-verify, failed, zombie, noise), writes the resulting rows through `orch unit add`, `orch unit set`, and `orch ledger record`, runs `orch status`, then spawns the next wave in one message.
- Account for every spawned worker at its track's rollup: arrived, respawned, or its scope explicitly absorbed. Silently redoing a missing worker's work hides both the wasted spend and the coverage gap its result existed to close.
- A drain turn ends with the three lines from `orch status`: counts against the states, what changed, gates open. Detail lives in `status.md`. The full reply contract applies at checkpoints and close.

#### Stack safety

- The frontier is a computed object, never narrative. Recompute `frontier.json` with `orch frontier set --repo <checkout>` after every merge and stack mutation: ordered PR list, branch names, head SHAs, a generation number, the lowest unmerged PR. The stacker's declared order in the store is authoritative, because GitHub base refs drift mid-restack. PR state and merged heads come from `gh api` REST, open heads from `git rev-parse` in the checkout, so fetch the stack's branches first. The command fails rather than guessing when an open PR's base ref doesn't match its parent or this checkout's head of an open PR isn't GitHub's. That failure is a stacker unit.
- Stacks are hand-chained base branches. Exactly one stacker per stack, a `poteto-agent` worker on Work, serialized within its stack, is the only worker allowed to rebase that stack or push it with `--force-with-lease`. Record the holder in the standing orders. Under a cloud lead, the lead pushes what the stacker rewrote.
- Workers never rebase. Babysitters follow `playbooks/babysit.md`, one per stack, scoped to one immutable frontier generation. They watch and report: conflicts to the stacker, fixes back to you as units. They never restack.
- PR closes and retargets go through the stacker only. Closing a base PR orphans every chain above it. Merges and stack surgery are units with briefs like any other. Branch deletions are human gates: no lead or worker deletes a remote branch.
- One retro watcher follows merged PRs for reverts, post-merge CI breaks, and orphaned follow-ups.

#### Verification

Scale verification to the unit. `VERIFY` is the worker's own targeted heavy commands, run through the machine lock, and the worker self-reports the result, which you record in the ledger. Spot-check its receipts. A dedicated verifier is for units whose verification is expensive, judgment-laden, or high-blast-radius: a fresh-context review on `poteto-reader` on the Judgement setting, briefed with the unit's PR, diff, acceptance criteria and `VERIFY`, never the worker's reasoning. It is a `poteto-reader` `Agent` with `isolation: "worktree"` whose brief names its start commit, on either surface. A verifier whose entire product would be rerunning one command is ceremony, not verification.

The full gate runs once per landing, on the integrated head, by whoever lands it (the lead, or a sub-lead for its track). Its result is the ledger row for that PR and head SHA. Workers never run it.

Write ledger rows with `orch ledger record`. Check the current PR and head SHA with `orch ledger check`. `ledger.tsv`, one row per verdict, keyed by PR number plus head SHA: `live-ui-verified | unit-test-verified | type-check-only | verifier-blocked | verifier-failed`. CI green is an input to a verdict, not a verdict. Behavioral work needs better than `type-check-only`. `verifier-blocked` is not a pass. Respawn when the environment heals. `verifier-failed` gets a fix unit, not a re-verify. A worker may self-report. A verifier overrides it on the same key, and the full gate overrides both, so record the gate's row last. A new head SHA voids the row, so re-verify after restack. The ledger answers "was this verified", not memory and not the transcript.

A unit is not done until its output is externalized the moment it lands, never batched to the end of the run. A worker commits its report to its own branch (a separate-session worker pushes it), a verifier's verdict reaches the ledger, receipts land in the store. Work that exists only on one VM when that VM dies was never done.

#### Liveness and failure

- Never resume a worker to check on it. A message to an idle worker wakes it. Probe read-only: the ledger, `units.tsv`, `gh` (`gh api` REST in cloud), pushed branches (`git ls-remote`), a `create_session` worker's `get_session`. Transcript mtime is not liveness.
- A worker stalled past 1.5× its `TIMEBOX` is `BLOCKED`, and gets no retry. Stop an `Agent` worker with `TaskStop`. For a separate-session worker, record its session URL, tell the user once, carry on and integrate without it.
- A silent death gets a synthetic postmortem row in the inbox (unit, failure mode, last evidence, options). Replan on evidence as it arrives. Never wait for full quiescence.
- Retry by failure mode. Cap-hit or out-of-memory, or a worker reporting `lock-starved`: replan into smaller units, not a retry. Network or infra drop: retry as-is, uncounted, bounded by the own-infra rule below. Tool error, unknown, or an `ISSUES` report: one retry on the same model and effort, then `BLOCKED` with evidence and a replan around the unit. Stalled past 1.5× the timebox: `BLOCKED`, as above.
- A zombie that returns hours late reconciles against the current frontier and ledger before anything is accepted. Salvage unique findings through a fresh unit, never a blind merge.
- When continued spawning would produce garbage tree-wide (bad upstream output, broken acceptance, dead infra), write a stop line at the top of the standing orders, let in-flight work finish, fix the cause, clear it.
- Bound your own infra retries the same way you bound a worker's. After a few consecutive tool aborts, stop retrying. Write a terminal handoff to durable state (what is done, where it lives, the exact command to resume) and end the run.
- **After a restart** (a new session, or `--resume`): in-VM and same-session workers are dead, separate-session workers (cloud workers, `create_session` workers) are not. Fetch the store branch (`git fetch origin pstack/orchestrate/<program-slug>`) and, when the checkout has no store, restore it with `mkdir -p .claude/pstack/orchestrate/<program-slug> && git archive FETCH_HEAD | tar -x -C .claude/pstack/orchestrate/<program-slug>`, then run `orch init`. Re-read the standing orders and `units.tsv`, recompute the frontier, reattach separate-session work by branch, PR and `get_session`, never by agent id, respawn one sub-lead per track from its stored brief plus current state, drain, resume. The dead session's store lock clears itself on the next write: `orch` replaces a lock whose holder pid is gone. A resume drops the reminder hook, so after `--resume` tell the user once to type `/pstack:poteto-mode` again.
- **After a compaction** (the same session goes on): in-VM and same-session workers are still running. Re-read the store and recompute the frontier before your next spawn, merge or push, but respawn nothing. Their completions arrive as before.

#### Escalation

Reaches the human, batched into the status page rather than per item: irreversible actions (force-push to shared branches, deploys, deletions, closing someone else's PR), genuine product or preference calls no experiment settles, a standing order that contradicts observed reality, a program-level dead end that survived a replan, a refused merge, work that needs the user's machine under a cloud lead. Park each with `orch gate park` before asking, and route work around it.

Never reaches the human: frontier nudges, restack mechanics, retries, CI flake triage, review-thread triage, format fixes, scope the brief already forbids (refuse and continue), and "should I keep going". When in doubt, act and log.

Mid-run discoveries fix only what blocks the frontier. Everything else parks in follow-ups. At this fan-out a small scope leak multiplies into PRs nobody asked for.

**Reply:** at checkpoints and close: the predicate and the count against it from `units.tsv` and `ledger.tsv`, tracks and what each landed, the frontier (PR list plus SHAs), verdicts summary, what was abandoned and why, gates awaiting the human (the only asks, each refused merge among them with its refusal text), the store path and its branch, and the trail path. Numbers from the tables, not narrative. Include PR links. At close, also list the leftover branches the run pushed: merged heads still present, and closed or abandoned unit branches. Leave out open-PR heads, and list the store branch as kept. Follow the list with one `git push origin --delete <branch> …` line for the user to run locally. Never delete a remote branch yourself.
